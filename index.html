Broken object-level authorization is the most common serious flaw in multi-tenant products and the hardest to catch, because every exploiting request is well-formed, authenticated, and returns a clean 200. No scanner finds it. The only reliable method is to enumerate the cross product of actors, objects and actions and compare what the system does against what policy says — which is what this runs, against a clinical platform where the objects are PHI.

How it is built. Policy in authz.js is written as predicates over (actor, object) rather than a role-permission grid, because nearly every real rule here is relational: a provider may read a note they authored for a client on their panel, and no amount of role checking expresses that. The harness treats policy as the oracle and the implementation as the thing under test, so a pass is not “no error” — it is “denied exactly what policy denies”. Severity is derived rather than assigned: PHI raises it, and PHI crossing a tenant boundary makes it critical.

The k-anonymity rule is the one worth arguing for. An employer admin can see utilization aggregates for their own population, but only above a cohort floor — below it a “report” tells an employer which of their employees sought mental health care. That is a privacy failure that every role check in the system would happily authorise, which is why it belongs in policy and in the harness rather than in someone's judgement. All actors, organizations and records are fabricated.
